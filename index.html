The craft

Made the way it has always been made.

Every piece comes from a living tradition, and often from a workshop where the same work has been done for generations. Here is what to look for.

Tamil Nadu
Temple jewellery

First made to adorn deities in South Indian temples, and now worn at weddings and festivals. Dense relief work with goddesses, peacocks and lotus motifs.

Rajasthan
Block printing

Patterns carved into wooden blocks and pressed onto cotton by hand, one colour at a time. Small shifts in the print are the mark of the hand.

Across India
Steel tiffins

The stacking lunch carrier that has gone to work, to school and onto trains for generations. Plain steel, no plastic, made to be used every day for decades.

The curator

About Roos

I have always been drawn to things that still carry the mark of the hands that made them: a bangle shaped over a flame in a family workshop, a length of cotton printed one wooden block at a time, a steel tiffin made to be carried to work every day for decades.

Wherever I can, I buy directly from the people who make the pieces, so I know where each one comes from and who shaped it. I choose every one as if I were choosing it for myself.

— Roos Follow the journey
